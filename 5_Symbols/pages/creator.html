A Changed Stance on Certification

Rifat didn't start here to collect a certificate and move on. He started here because he was afraid of the exam.

That fear became the engine. Instead of treating certification as a finish line — something you pass once, frame on the wall, and forget — he shifted to a completely different model: continuous certification. The goal is not a credential. The goal is the habit of showing up, studying, and staying current in a field that rewrites itself every few months.

😰

Fear as Fuel

The anxiety of sitting an AI exam is real. Rather than avoiding it, Rifat chose to walk toward it repeatedly — using the discomfort of testing as proof that he is still growing, not coasting.

🔁

Continuous, Not One-Off

Certifications expire. Skills drift. The commitment is to re-certify, re-study, and rebuild understanding whenever the landscape shifts — treating AI credentials the way software treats version numbers: always incrementing.

🧠

AI Skills + Self-Learning Skills Together

Every certification cycle trains two things at once: the AI content itself, and the meta-skill of learning how to learn. The study app, the memory cards, the practice exams — they are as much about building a learning system as they are about passing a test.

🚀

Breaking Out of Fixed Roles

Developer. Ops. DevOps. SRE. These were real roles — and real limitations. The AI era doesn't reward those who master a fixed identity. It rewards those who can move fluidly between problem spaces. Rifat is deliberately refusing to be pinned to any single lane.

🌱

Transformation as Self-Care

Staying relevant is not just a career strategy — it is a form of looking after yourself. Getting stuck in a role the world has moved past is stressful. Continuously learning with AI is how Rifat keeps his work meaningful, his options open, and his energy directed forward.

🤝

Learning With AI, Not Just About AI

The distinction matters. Not studying AI as an outside observer, but using AI tools — Claude, Gemini, Kimi, DeepSeek — as active partners in the learning process itself. The certification is a waypoint. The partnership with AI is ongoing.

"I don't want to be a Dev who learned AI. I don't want to be an Ops person who learned AI. I want to be someone who transforms with AI — and builds the habit of never stopping that transformation." — Rifat Erdem Sahin
