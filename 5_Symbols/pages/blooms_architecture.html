🧠 Bloom's Learning Architecture

A self-aware study system built on Bloom's Taxonomy. Each stage has a role — memory cards anchor the foundation, questioning builds understanding, analysis draws the map, evaluation motivates, and story-writing holds it all together.

🔮 The Meta-Skill: Being Aware of the Process

Most learning happens to you. This system asks you to learn about learning. At each stage, you pause and ask: "Where am I in the loop? What does this stage demand? Am I recalling, connecting, or creating?" That awareness transforms passive study into active construction of knowledge.

Memory cards are not just flashcards — they are the living record of the loop. Every time you cycle through a stage and find a gap, a new card is born. The deck grows with your understanding, not against a fixed syllabus.

🧠 Stage 1  ·  Foundation
Remember

The foundation of all learning. Memory cards create a living, growing library of facts, patterns, and associations. Each pass through the cycle adds new cards when gaps are found — the deck is never finished because learning never stops.

Flip cards back and forth to build retrieval strength
Add a new card every time you discover a gap
Spaced repetition keeps old cards from decaying
Awareness check: "Am I recognising or truly recalling?"
↓
❓ Stage 2  ·  Comprehension
Understand

Questioning is the engine of understanding. You don't passively read — you interrogate. Can you recall the answer correctly? Can you reason through it without the card? This stage builds the mental model that makes the next stages possible.

Ask "why does this work?" not just "what is this?"
Correct recall strengthens the memory trace
Reason through the answer before checking
Awareness check: "Can I explain this in my own words?"
↓
🔗 Stage 3  ·  Connection
Analyse

Analysis is synthesis at scale. You take many separate understandings and draw the map that connects them. Patterns emerge. Dependencies become visible. The picture you draw here is the cognitive architecture of the domain — your own mental model.

Group concepts by theme, dependency, or contrast
Draw diagrams, mind maps, or architecture charts
Explain how concept A causes or enables concept B
Awareness check: "Can I see the whole picture at once?"
↓
⚖️ Stage 4  ·  Judgement
Evaluate

Evaluation is where motivation lives. You weigh the pros and cons, decide what matters, and connect insights across multiple analyses. Communicating your judgement — even to yourself — makes it real. This stage fuels the energy to reach the Create stage.

State the pros and cons of each approach or concept
Ask "why should I care?" — find the personal relevance
Connect judgements across multiple analysis maps
Awareness check: "Am I motivated to go further?"
↓
✍️ Stage 5  ·  Synthesis
Create

The story is the container that holds everything together. Writing forces clarity. When you weave Remember (the facts), Understand (the reasoning), Analyse (the connections), and Evaluate (the judgements) into a single narrative, the knowledge becomes genuinely yours — durable, transferable, and alive.

Write the story of what you learned — in your own voice
Reference the memory cards as plot points in the narrative
The story is the proof — if you can't tell it, you don't own it
Awareness check: "Does my story make sense to a newcomer?"
